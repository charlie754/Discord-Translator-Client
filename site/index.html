TimeAbout 25 minutes
CostUSD 0 a month if you do step 9

Do you actually need this?

Probably not. Discord Translator works out of the box with its default Google (free) provider — no account, no card, no key.

Three honest reasons to want your own key:

  • The free endpoint is unofficial and can rate-limit or break without notice.
  • No quota guarantee, so heavy use can simply start failing.
  • Nobody is accountable — no SLA, no support.

Free routes exist: this plugin’s default provider, the DeepL free tier, and a Google Apps Script proxy — a known free route other projects use, which we do not ship or support.

Stop here and use the free provider, or continue to step 1.

Three things that cost people money here

1. A Cloud Billing budget does not cap spending

“Setting an alerts-only budget doesn’t automatically cap Google Cloud or Google Maps Platform usage or spending.” — Google

A budget sends you an email. It stops nothing. By the time the email arrives the money is already spent.

Set one anyway as a second signal. Never treat it as protection.

Aside: Google also has spend cap budgets, which do stop usage. They are in Preview and Cloud Translation is not confirmed as an eligible service. Do not rely on them.

2. A new project has no daily character limit at all

The default characters-per-day quota is unlimited. The only limit applied out of the box is a rate limit of 6,000,000 characters per minute. At USD 20.00 per 1,000,000 characters that is:

USD 120 per minute6,000,000 ÷ 1,000,000 × USD 20 USD 7,200 per hour USD 172,800 per dayThe worst case for a leaked key you never capped

That is arithmetic, not a scare figure: the published default rate limit multiplied by the published price.

3. The only hard stop is the quota page

Exceeding a quota you set returns HTTP 403 and Google rejects the request instead of billing it. Nothing else in this guide does that.

That is step 9. It is the reason this page exists. Do not skip it.

There is no free tier on the Cloud Translation API — this one API, not Google translation in general. There is a monthly credit of up to USD 10, which covers roughly 500,000 characters. Google’s wording: “Credit usage applies collectively to both Cloud Translation - Basic and Cloud Translation - Advanced. The credit is up to $10, based on your usage and does not roll over.”

It is a credit, not a wall. Cross it and nothing refuses you — the next character costs USD 20.00 per 1,000,000, charged to your card without being asked again.

What exactly you are billed for — characters, whitespace, placeholders
  • Code points, not bytes. Google: “Cloud Translation counts usage on a per character basis, even if a character is multiple bytes. Each character corresponds to a code point.” Japanese and Chinese are not charged three times over.
  • Everything you send, whitespace included. Google: “You are charged for all characters that you include in a Cloud Translation request, even untranslated characters. This includes, for example, whitespace characters.”
  • Empty queries are charged too. Discord Translator does not send them. The rule is Google’s, so do not send empty strings if you test the API by hand.
  • The placeholders this plugin substitutes. Links, mentions and custom emoji are replaced with a short marker before sending. Those characters are billed. They are almost always fewer than what they replaced.
The local cache helps, and it stops helping at 5,000 messages

Translations are cached locally in a 5,000-entry LRU, keyed by a hash of the message text plus the target language. It survives restarting Discord.

  • Re-reading something still in the cache does not re-send it. That saving is real.
  • Past 5,000 distinct messages the oldest are discarded, and re-reading those re-bills. It looks exactly like ordinary scrolling.
  • 5,000 messages of average length is about 300,000 characters — so eviction starts before the monthly credit runs out, not after.
  • Changing your target language re-translates everything: the language is part of the cache key.
  • Double- and triple-click selection translation is not cached at all.

So caching does not make re-reading free. The quota cap in step 9 is the only ceiling on your bill.

Two Google hostnames that look like typos of each other

People search the console for the wrong one, find nothing, and assume something is broken.

  • translation.googleapis.com — the host your key’s requests are actually sent to.
  • translate.googleapis.com — the service name inside Google Cloud Console, which is what you search for and what the quota page is filtered by.

Different strings, both correct, in different places. The deep links on this page already use the right one for each page.

1

Sign in to Google Cloud Console

About 2 minutes

Open the console and sign in with your Google account. Accept the terms when asked.

Open Google Cloud Console ↗

If you already use Gmail, YouTube or Drive, you already have the account. Cloud is a separate activation on it.

About the free trial credit — it is not the translation credit

Google usually offers new Cloud customers a one-off trial credit. The published figure has been USD 300 over 90 days; check Google’s own page rather than this one, because it changes.

  • The trial credit is not the monthly Translation credit. The Translation allowance is a recurring monthly credit of up to USD 10.
  • The trial ending does not delete your key or your project. It changes where the overage goes: your card instead of the trial balance.
  • The trial does not remove the requirement to enable billing. That is step 4.

Why a separate project matters

A project is a container. Keys, enabled APIs, quotas and bills all belong to one. Give this its own project and its quota and its bill are about Discord Translator alone — which is what makes the cap in step 9 meaningful and the billing report in step 12 readable.

3

Check the top bar shows your new project

About 1 minute

Look at the project selector in the top bar of the console. If it does not show discord-translator, click it and pick that project.

Every step after this one applies to whichever project is selected there.

Why this one-minute check is here at all

Doing steps 4 to 9 against the wrong project is the single most common way this goes wrong, and the symptom is nasty: you enable the API on project A, create a key in project B, and get a 403 that reads exactly like a bad key. You then spend an hour debugging a key that was fine.

Not willing to put a card on file? That is a reasonable position — stop here and take one of the free routes listed at the top of this page.

Why skipping this looks exactly like a bad key

Every Google API is off by default. A project with billing enabled and a perfectly good key still returns HTTP 403 until the Translation API is switched on for that specific project. The error names the project, not the key, but people read “403” and go re-copy the key.

6

Create an API key

About 1 minute

Open Credentials, use + Create credentials at the top, choose API key, and copy the key from the dialog.

Open Credentials ↗
https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/credentials

Do not close the dialog and walk away. Go straight to step 7. An unrestricted key can be used for anything in your project, by anyone who gets it, up to whatever your card will absorb.

You can always come back: the same credentials page, then click the key’s name.

Treat the key like a password. Anyone holding it can spend your quota, billed to your card. Never paste it into a chat, an issue, a screenshot or a repository.

7

Restrict the key to the Cloud Translation API only

About 2 minutes

On the key’s own page, find API restrictions, then:

  1. Choose to restrict the key rather than leaving it unrestricted.
  2. Tick Cloud Translation API and nothing else.
  3. Save.
Open Credentials, then click your key ↗

Now the key is useless for anything except translation.

This is one of the two things actually protecting you

If the key leaks, the worst an attacker can do is spend your translation quota — bad, but bounded. Without this restriction a leaked key reaches every API you ever enable on that project.

The other protection is the quota cap in step 9. Between them they are the whole defence, because — as step 8 explains — none of Google’s application restrictions fit this app.

8

Leave Application restrictions set to None

About 1 minute

On the same page, find Application restrictions and make sure it is set to None. Save if you changed it.

This is a step where the right answer is to leave something alone. Setting “Websites” here will break your key.

Why none of Google’s four options work for this app

Google offers exactly four application restrictions: Websites (HTTP referrers), IP addresses, Android apps, iOS apps.

  • Websites looks like exactly what you want and is the trap. In the desktop build the request is made from Electron’s main process, which is not a web page. In the browser extension it is made from the extension’s background context, which is the only part allowed to make cross-origin requests and which carries no page referrer. Google sees no referrer, finds no match in your list, and refuses. The error reads “Requests from referer <empty> are blocked”, which is baffling when you never set a referrer.
  • IP addresses needs a fixed public IP that never changes. Most home connections do not have one, and you would be re-editing this every time it moved.
  • Android apps and iOS apps are not what you are running.

So the protection is step 7 plus step 9, and that is a real trade-off you should know you are making: the key will work from wherever it is used. Which is precisely why the cap matters.

One qualification: the userscript and plain-web bundles — not what the README tells you to install — fetch directly from the discord.com page, so they can carry a referrer. Even there it is fragile and it is not what protects you from a leak. Test it before relying on it.

Paste the key into Discord Translator

About 2 minutes

In Discord, open Settings (the cog by your username, bottom left) → Plugins → ChannelTranslator → the cog on that plugin’s entry.

Paste your key into this field first:

googleCloudApiKey

Then set Provider to:

Google Cloud Translation (your own key)

Key first, provider second.

Why the key goes in before the provider

Discord Translator refuses to use a key-requiring provider with no key, and shows a notice explaining why rather than failing silently on every message. That notice is correct and doing its job — but switch the provider first and you trigger it on your own half-finished configuration, then have to work out that nothing is actually wrong.

11

Set your target language

About 1 minute

On the same settings screen, set targetLanguage to the language you want.

targetLanguage

The default is English. If you wanted something else and left it alone, everything will work perfectly and translate into English — which looks exactly like setup having failed.

It is a dropdown of 15 languages, not a text box. ZH-TW — 繁體中文 and ZH-CN — 简体中文 are separate entries, because “Chinese” is not one language code.

The spend meter and the monthly cap you can see under the key

Two controls sit directly beneath googleCloudApiKey. Neither is a Google control and neither replaces step 9.

The spend meter is a read-only panel headed Spend meter — YYYY-MM in your own local calendar month. It counts only the providers that bill you — Google Cloud and DeepL — in code points, using the exact text handed to the provider. The free Google provider is never counted. A money figure is shown only for Google Cloud. It shows how much of the USD 10 credit has been spent and how much is left, and past the credit it shows the estimated amount billed. Clearing it takes two clicks: the first arms the button, the second erases the month.

It is an estimate and it cannot match Google’s invoice. It sees only what this plugin sent, so anything else on the same billing account spending the same shared credit is invisible to it. Google also charges for empty queries, which have no characters to count. A network timeout is counted as sent, deliberately, because a spend cap must err upward.

The monthly character cap is the setting monthlyCharacterCap and it ships as 0, meaning no cap. Until you type a number into it, nothing on that screen stops a single request. Set a number and, from the next message on, the plugin adds up what has gone to paid providers this month plus what this request would add; if the total would cross your cap, nothing is sent. It refuses the whole message rather than half of it, tells you the refusal is your own setting rather than an error from Google, and clears itself when the month rolls over.

What that cap cannot do: it runs inside your own Discord client, so it cannot stop a leaked key — someone else spending your key does it from their machine, which never sees your settings. Only the quota in step 9 can refuse that. It also cannot un-bill anything already sent, and it counts characters, not money.

There are two language controls and they are one setting

The targetLanguage dropdown in settings and the Target Language dropdown on the translator panel offer the same 15 entries and write the same value. Changing either changes both. Use whichever is in front of you.

Changing the language re-translates what is on screen, and each language is cached separately — so switching back and forth costs characters both ways.

12

Turn translation on and check it works

About 2 minutes

Open a server channel — not a DM, where the panel is hidden by design. Look at the top-right of the chat area for the translator panel and use its toggle.

There is no toolbar button. The panel is the only control.

Working looks like: the panel reads Translating… and then On, and messages appear in your target language.

A second way to see it working: double-click a message and the translation of the selected text appears in a popover above it.

If it does not work

Test the key on its own before changing any setting. This gives you Google’s error word for word instead of a symptom.

Windows, in PowerShell

$key = "PASTE_YOUR_KEY_HERE"
Invoke-RestMethod -Method Post -Uri "https://translation.googleapis.com/language/translate/v2?key=$key" `
  -ContentType "application/json" `
  -Body '{"q":"hello","target":"es","format":"text"}'

macOS or Linux, in a terminal

curl -s -X POST \
  "https://translation.googleapis.com/language/translate/v2?key=PASTE_YOUR_KEY_HERE" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"q":"hello","target":"es","format":"text"}'

A working key returns:

{"data":{"translations":[{"translatedText":"hola"}]}}

Anything else is an error from Google, and it names its own cause. The three most common:

Symptom → cause → fix.
What you seeWhat it meansFix
Cloud Translation API has not been used in project … before or it is disabled (HTTP 403) The API is not enabled on this project. This is the commonest failure and it looks exactly like a bad key. Step 5. Check the project selector was showing the right project, then wait two minutes.
API key not valid. Please pass a valid API key. (HTTP 400) The key is mistyped, truncated, or has been deleted. Re-copy it from the credentials page. Check for a trailing space.
Requests from referer <empty> are blocked (HTTP 403) You set a Websites (HTTP referrer) restriction on the key. Step 8. Set Application restrictions to None.

An HTTP 403 has more than one cause. It means either the API is not enabled on the project (step 5) or the key is restricted in a way that blocks the request (steps 7 and 8). Read the message text, not the number.

Inside Discord, a run of failed requests makes the panel read Rate limited and translation pauses. The panel cannot show you Google’s message, so run the test command above — it is the fastest way to find out which row you are in.

If your key leaks: delete it. Never rotate it.

Rotating a leaked key does not stop the leaked key

Google’s rotation feature creates a new key. In Google’s words the old key string “remains active during the transition period” — it keeps working until you manually delete it, and there is no automatic expiration.

Rotate a leaked key and stop there, and the leaked value is still spending your money.

Do this immediately if the key has been shared, pasted anywhere public, committed to a repository, or was in the browser extension on a machine you no longer trust.

  1. Open Credentials.
  2. Select the compromised key and use the control named roughly Delete.
  3. Create a fresh key with step 6, restrict and cap it with steps 7 to 9, and paste it in with step 10.
Open Credentials to delete the key ↗

A deleted key stops working immediately. Google keeps it recoverable for 30 days in case you deleted the wrong one, after which it is gone for good. That window is a safety net for your mistakes, not a reason to delay.

Afterwards, check your billing reports filtered to the Cloud Translation API to see whether anything was spent that you did not do yourself.

Where your key is stored, and why the browser extension is riskier

In the browser extension your key is stored in discord.com localStorage, where any script running on that page can read it. A stolen Google Cloud key can be billed to your card.

The desktop build is the safer place for a paid key: settings are a file in the app’s own data directory, not storage belonging to a web page you are logged into.

If you use the browser extension anyway — a reasonable choice, and your call — the quota cap in step 9 is your backstop, and it is the only thing between a leaked key and an unbounded bill. Set it low. You can raise it later in a minute.

Stopping without a leak

Delete the key as above, then clear the googleCloudApiKey field in Discord Translator and set Provider back to Google (free) or DeepL (your own key).

Do one more thing

Open the quotas page and confirm your characters-per-day limit is showing the number you set. That single check is what stands between a leaked key and a five-figure bill.
